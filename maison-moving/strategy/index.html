The short version

Where the two profiles stand now, and what to focus on next.
  • Two profiles, two different races. Chino Hills is the #1 mover across the Inland Empire. Tustin sits mid-pack in a crowded Orange County market and is climbing back from a spring dip. About 73% of all tracked calls come straight from these two Google Maps profiles, so Maps is the lead engine.
  • The single biggest fix is connecting the website to both profiles. The site still calls itself "Maison Moving (Staging)" in its code, carries no business or location markup that names the addresses and numbers, and leans on a shared toll-free line. That quietly limits how high both pins can climb.
  • Point the Chino Hills profile at its own Chino Hills page (today it points at the homepage), and turn the Tustin and Chino Hills pages into real location pages (Google currently reads them as blog posts).
  • Do not build more neighborhood pages. The site already has a large set of thin "moving company in [neighborhood]" pages that add little. The work is to strengthen the few real location and service pages that already exist, and retire the obvious duplicates.
  • Add a few pages for the high-value specialty jobs they already do, piano and antiques, senior and estate moves, and office moves, judged by jobs booked rather than search volume.

The frame

Chino Hills already dominates the Inland Empire and Tustin is competitive in Orange County, but both profiles are barely connected to the website, so the highest-leverage move is wiring the site to both pins, not building new pages.

Maison runs two managed Google profiles with opposite stories. Chino Hills holds #1 across its Inland-Empire map grid (the widest top-3 coverage of any mover there, ahead of Allied Van Lines and Berger Allied), and it is still extending that lead. Tustin is mid-pack in a much denser Orange County market and is recovering from an early-May dip. Roughly 73% of tracked phone calls come from these two Maps profiles, which makes Maps the lead engine for the whole business. The site itself ranks for about 127 search terms, with page-one wins on the brand name and on Chino Hills "movers" searches, and organic clicks hit a 12-month high in May. The problem is the connective tissue: the site does not identify its two locations to Google in structured data, and it still carries a "(Staging)" name in its code. So the play is: connect the site to both profiles, make the two location pages real, strengthen the pages that already exist, add a little specialty depth, and keep the measurement clean. It is not rebuilding the neighborhood-page grid.

Search volumes are DataForSEO estimates, not literal counts. Both Tustin and Chino Hills are managed HQDM pins, so both are in scope here.

The two profiles, side by side

Chino Hills wins a less-crowded Inland-Empire field on a small but rising review base; Tustin has the bigger reputation but fights a dense Orange County pack and slipped before recovering.

  • Chino Hills (4.8 rating, 133 reviews): #1 on its grid with the widest top-3 coverage of any mover in the Inland Empire, leading at roughly a 1.65× margin over Allied Van Lines, with 2Man Moving and Berger Allied behind. It added about 60 top-3 map spots between early April and early May and is still gaining. The competition here is mostly national van-line agents, not strong local operators, which is why a profile with only 133 reviews can lead.
  • Tustin HQ (4.6 rating, 858 reviews): a much harder market. The grid is led by Pro Movers (193 top-3 spots), Universal Moving and Best Fit Movers, with Maison in the next group at 133 and recovering after an early-May dip. Tustin has by far the bigger review base, but several rivals sit at 4.9 to 5.0, so the rating is the softer signal here.
  • What it means: defend and extend Chino Hills, and keep lifting Tustin out of its dip. The lever for both is the same: the Maps profile and how well the website backs it up.

Where each pin is strong and weak on the map

Chino Hills is top-3 almost everywhere on its grid; Tustin is strongest on the "near me" and storage-and-packing terms and thinner on the bare "movers" search, with the weak spots being distance from each pin rather than a per-keyword gap.

  • Chino Hills: top-3 across almost the entire grid on every tracked term, with an average position under 3. The strongest searches are "commercial moving" and "office moving services," where it gained the most ground in the spring scans. There is very little headroom left here; the job is to hold it.
  • Tustin: the clearest gains came on "packing and storage near me" and "long distance moving near me," which both jumped in the May scans. The bare term "movers" and "local moving" are the thinner ones. Average position is around 8 to 9, so the pin is present across the grid but rarely top-3 yet, which is the recovery opportunity.
  • The weak spots are distance, not keywords: each pin fades at the edges of its grid, away from the storefront. That is proximity, which a landing page does not fix. The fix is prominence (a stronger, better-connected profile) and, for the far edges, organic pages rather than the map pack.
  • Watch list: in Chino Hills, Allied Van Lines, Amigos Moving and 2Man Moving all added top-3 spots in the spring, so the lead is being chased. In Tustin, Pro Movers and the newer entrants (CC Relocation, Costa Mesa Moving) are the ones to track.

The website-to-profile connection (the core gap)

The site and the two profiles read like separate assets: no business or location markup, a leftover "(Staging)" name, location pages coded as blog posts, and the Chino Hills profile pointing at the homepage. Repairing this is the highest-return work in the engagement.
Learn moreShow less▾

Checked live in June 2026, the website does almost nothing to tell Google it runs two real locations. The fixes are concrete, mostly one-time, and mostly developer-side:

  • No business or location markup anywhere. The structured data is only a generic Organization, a WebSite, and Article objects. There is no MovingCompany or per-location LocalBusiness carrying the addresses, geo-coordinates, hours, links to the two Maps listings, or the review rating. Google has to guess that the site and the profiles are the same business.
  • The brand name in the code still says "Maison Moving (Staging)." That is the name Google reads in the page metadata and structured data on every page. It should be "Maison Moving & Storage."
  • The two location pages are coded as blog posts. Both /tustin/ and /chino-hills/ are real, useful pages, but they are marked up as articles (author listed as info@hqdm.io), carry no Google map, and do not link in-body to the service pages. Google reads them as blog entries, not service locations.
  • The Chino Hills profile points at the homepage. A dedicated /chino-hills/ page already exists, but the profile sends its clicks to the homepage with a tracking tag instead of to that page.
  • Numbers and consistency. The site leans on a shared toll-free line while each profile uses its own local number. With call tracking (CallRail) in place, some difference between the displayed number and the profile number is normal and fine for measuring calls. The goal is not to chase every number, it is to make the structured data for each location carry a clear local number consistent with its Google profile.
  • Duplicate city pages. /serving-all-of-tustin-ca/ and /serving-all-of-chino-hills-ca/ overlap directly with /tustin/ and /chino-hills/. Pick one page per city and redirect the weaker one.

Operational (measurement and details)

CallRail is the real record of leads and shows Maps driving about 73% of calls, but that is not reflected in Google Analytics yet, and the founding year is inconsistent across sources.
Learn moreShow less▾

The leads that matter are phone calls, and CallRail is the system that records them. It shows a steady volume of tracked calls a month with about 73% coming from the two Maps profiles. Two cleanups make the reporting trustworthy. First, that CallRail record should drive the monthly report, because Google Analytics is not set up to count leads: the form, phone and email events fire but are not marked as conversions, and phone tracking in Analytics only switched on around March 2026, so the Analytics numbers understate and mis-time the real call volume. Second, the founding year is inconsistent (the tagline says 1984, the Tustin profile says 1992, the Chino Hills profile says 1996); one confirmed year strengthens the about page, the markup and every directory listing.

What to track

  • Use CallRail as the lead number in the monthly report (first-time callers, and the share from each Maps profile), not the Analytics event counts.
  • Mark the form, phone-click and email-click events as key events in Google Analytics so the two systems agree over time.
  • Keep both Maps grids on the Local Dominator schedule so Chino Hills' lead and Tustin's recovery stay visible month to month.

The strategies

1

Connect the website to both profiles

the prerequisite
Add business and per-location markup, fix the "(Staging)" name, and surface both addresses, so the site finally tells Google it runs the Tustin and Chino Hills locations.
Learn moreShow less▾
What
Deploy MovingCompany plus one LocalBusiness object per location, each with the correct address, geo-coordinates, hours, a link to that location's Google Maps listing and the brand's social profiles, and the review rating. Replace "Maison Moving (Staging)" in the site title, the page metadata and the markup with "Maison Moving & Storage." Show both locations' name, address and local number in the footer and on a simple locations page. Set the page author off info@hqdm.io.
Why
Nothing else in Maps compounds until the site identifies its two locations to Google. Today the only structured data is a generic Organization (named "Staging"), a WebSite and Article objects, with no address, phone, geo or link to either Maps listing. That forfeits entity clarity and quietly caps both pins, the Tustin pin most of all, because it needs every edge in a dense market. This is almost entirely one-time developer work, so it is the highest return for the effort in the whole plan.
How
Roll a schema template across the homepage and both location pages: MovingCompany for the brand, a LocalBusiness per pin with NAP, geo, areaServed, sameAs to both Maps URLs and the socials, and aggregateRating. Fix the WordPress site title and the social-share name in the same pass. Add the footer and locations hub. One developer sprint covers most of it.
2

Make the two location pages real, and repoint the Chino Hills profile

location axis
Re-type the Tustin and Chino Hills pages from blog posts into location pages with a map and local number, and point the Chino Hills profile at its own page.
Learn moreShow less▾
What
Convert /tustin/ and /chino-hills/ from blog Article markup to proper location pages: embed each pin's Google map, show that location's local number, add a short FAQ, and link in-body to the service pages. Then point the Chino Hills profile's website link to /chino-hills/ instead of the homepage.
Why
Both pages already exist and read well, but Google treats them as blog entries (author info@hqdm.io, no map, no in-body service links). Re-typing them and wiring the Chino Hills profile to its own page is the cheapest way to give each pin a real landing target. The map embed itself is not a ranking factor, it is a trust and contact block, but the page type, the local number and the in-body links all are signals.
How
Swap the JSON-LD to a location-page type tied to the matching LocalBusiness from Strategy 1, add the map and FAQ, add the "our services" link block, and update the Chino Hills profile link in GBP Manager once the page carries its local number.
3

Strengthen what exists; do not expand the neighborhood grid

page discipline
Keep the page effort on the real service pages and the two location pages, and retire the obvious duplicates instead of adding more thin neighborhood pages.
Learn moreShow less▾
What
Strengthen the service pages that already rank (/localmoving/, /cross-countrylong-distance-moving/, /commercialoffice-moving/, /office-movers/, /storage/) with the local signals and markup from Strategy 1. Redirect the duplicate city pages (/serving-all-of-tustin-ca/ and /serving-all-of-chino-hills-ca/) into /tustin/ and /chino-hills/. Do not add to the thin "[adjective] moving company in [neighborhood]" set the site already carries.
Why
The site already has a large set of thin neighborhood pages and a pair of exact-duplicate city pages. Adding more of that pattern risks looking like doorway pages to Google and dilutes the pages that matter. The commercial wins today are concentrated on the brand and the Chino Hills and Tustin "movers" terms, so the highest return is making those few pages stronger, not spreading effort across a wider grid.
How
Pick one canonical page per city and 301 the weaker duplicate. Give each kept service page the map, local section and "[service] tustin / chino hills" wording, paired with its LocalBusiness entry. Leave the existing neighborhood pages in place but stop expanding them; revisit the weakest only if they actively hurt.

The next layer: depth, not more pages

Once the foundation is fixed, the remaining gains come from a little specialty depth and from keeping the two profiles consistent, not from building out the grid.
Learn moreShow less▾

With the site connected to both profiles and the location pages real, the matrix stays consolidated. The last two strategies add depth where it pays: a few unique pages for the high-value specialty jobs Maison already does, and an alignment pass so the two profiles and the site all tell the same story. Page effort goes into those and into the pages that already rank, not back into the neighborhood cross-product.

4

Cover the high-value specialty jobs

service axis
Add unique pages for the specialty work Maison performs but barely mentions: piano and antiques, senior and estate moves, and office moves, judged by jobs booked.
Learn moreShow less▾
What
Add a focused page for each high-value specialty job: piano and antiques, senior and estate moves, and commercial/office (strengthen the existing /office-movers/ rather than starting fresh). For long distance, optimize the existing /cross-countrylong-distance-moving/ page; do not build a second one.
Why
These are real services with high value per job and clear buyer intent, even at low search volume. They also let the site match the profiles' service lists. The point is a few unique, genuinely useful pages, not a templated set, so each is judged by jobs booked rather than search volume, where even a handful a year pays off.
How
Audit the specialty work Maison actually does, add one page per real service at matched depth, link each from the relevant service and location pages, and keep one clear call to action per page. Tie each to its GBP service entry.
5

Unify the two profiles and keep measurement clean

consistency
Align the two profiles' category, name and details, confirm one founding year, and make CallRail the lead number in the report.
Learn moreShow less▾
What
Set both profiles' primary category to "Mover," align the name, hours and service lists across the two, confirm one founding year across the site and both profiles, and switch the monthly report to CallRail for the lead number while marking the Analytics lead events as key events.
Why
The two profiles use different category labels today ("Removals company" style on one, "Moving and storage service" on the other), and the founding year disagrees across sources. Consistency across the two pins is itself a ranking and trust signal, and a clean lead number keeps the reporting honest, since Analytics is not set up to count leads.
How
Make the category and detail edits in GBP Manager for both pins, confirm the founding year with the owner, wire the CallRail account into the monthly report, and flag the Analytics lead events as key events in one pass.
