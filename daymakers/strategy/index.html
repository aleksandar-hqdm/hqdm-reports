The short version

Where this brand stands now, and what to focus on next.
  • Strong business: one of the biggest review counts in their markets (2,265 reviews at 4.9 to 5.0 stars) and 4 of their 5 locations sit at #1 on their local map.
  • The problem is aim. They win the exact searches almost nobody types, and lose the popular "movers near me" searches that bring in the calls.
  • Focus: chase those popular searches across each location's wider area, the way their Hudson location already does and wins everywhere.
  • Fix the one weak spot, Eau Claire: its Google profile points to the wrong page and it has very few reviews compared to the others.
  • Clean up the low-quality links dragging the site down, and aim the monthly page work at the popular searches instead of tiny neighborhood pages that nobody finds.

The frame

Daymakers wins the low-volume searches and the area right next to each office, but loses the popular "near me" searches that drive the calls. The plan: win those across each pin's wider service area (Hudson already does), fix Eau Claire, and clean up the links, re-aiming the page work rather than adding more suburb pages.

Daymakers is a strong, established five-pin network with one of the largest review bases in its markets (2,265 reviews at 4.9 to 5.0) and four of five locations leading their local map. But the keyword and map data show a clear inversion. They dominate the service-plus-city terms that almost nobody searches (movers roseville, local movers lake elmo: 0 to 390 a month) and the area right next to each office, while they lose the generic "movers near me / moving company / local movers" terms that carry the demand (49,000 to 301,000 a month) across the wider service area. Those generic searches are the Maps-pack queries that drive about 88% of the CallRail leads. So the play is not more zero-volume suburb pages (58 already exist and together draw about 7 clicks across 90 days); it is to point that same monthly page production at the generic-term ring across each pin's service area (the Hudson model, already proven), fix Eau Claire from the ground up (its profile points at the homepage and it has 22 reviews against roughly 420 to 730 elsewhere), clean up a spam-dragged link profile, and re-aim content from suburbs to the terms with real demand.

Search volumes are DataForSEO estimates, not literal counts, and localize to each metro. All GBP work is the five managed pins, re-pointing their service areas, not new locations.

The competitive picture

The field is won on reviews and proximity, not content or links. Daymakers has the best ratings and the most links, so its edge losses split into two causes: a real review gap (Eau Claire, Roseville) and physically closer small movers (Bloomington, Lake Elmo).

The moving field here is local and won mostly on reviews and proximity, not content or links. Daymakers has the best ratings (4.9 to 5.0) and more links than any local rival, so it is not a quality problem. The edge losses split into two different causes that need two different fixes:

  • Prominence (reviews), where the gap is real. Eau Claire: Elite Movers (1,171 reviews) and Dan the Moving Man (878) own the market against Daymakers' 22. Roseville: the metro brands out-review Daymakers' 457, Good Stuff (1,810), You Move Me (1,696), College Hunks (2,202), Two Men and a Truck (906), and win the wider area on prominence over proximity.
  • Proximity, where Daymakers already leads on reviews. Bloomington (729) and Lake Elmo (422) out-review their competitors (Minne Movers 210, WellRive 119, Minute Men 104; Two Men 679, Revival 110), so the edge losses there are to physically closer small movers, not stronger profiles. The fix is service-area signals, not reviews.
  • Hudson is the proof. With 635 reviews against a field whose nearest rival has 74, Hudson leads every search across its whole area. Where Daymakers has the review lead, it wins.

Authority twist: Daymakers has the most backlinks of the local field (1,229) but a weaker domain authority than Elite Movers, Dan, and Good Stuff, who rank stronger with a fraction of the links. The profile is dragged down by spam (a likely residue of the halted off-page program). Quantity without quality. Good Stuff also ranks for more keywords (876 vs 657) with content aimed at real metro terms.

The strategies

1

Win the generic searches across each service-area ring

where the calls are
Shift the local effort from the service-plus-city terms (won, near-zero volume) to the generic "near me" terms across each pin's wider service area; they drive about 88% of the calls, and Hudson already wins them edge to edge.

What
Shift the local effort from the service-plus-city terms (won, near-zero volume) to the generic terms (movers near me, moving company, local movers) across the wider service area of each pin, not just next to the office.
Why
The generic terms carry the demand (49,000 to 301,000 versus 0 to 390 for the service-plus-city ones) and they are the Maps-pack queries that drive about 88% of leads. Daymakers ranks at the top for them in each grid core but slips to #5 to #12 at the edges, where a competitor takes the call. Hudson already wins these edge to edge, so it is proven achievable.
How
Apply the Hudson model to Roseville, Lake Elmo, and Bloomington: a strong review base plus a complete city page plus full GBP service-area signals. Extend each profile's service area outward; for the proximity-driven pins (Bloomington, Lake Elmo) this is the main lever. Track the generic terms across the grid, not just the service-plus-city ones.
2

Fix Eau Claire from the ground up

the one weak market
Eau Claire is the weakest pin: its Google profile points at the homepage and it has 22 reviews against several hundred elsewhere. Repoint the profile to the existing Eau Claire page, deepen it, and grow the reviews.
Learn moreShow less▾
What
Repoint the Eau Claire Google profile from the homepage to /eau-claire-wi-movers/, deepen that existing page with the Chippewa Valley suburbs (Chippewa Falls, Altoona, Menomonie), and grow the review base from 22 toward the level of the other markets.
Why
Eau Claire is the weakest pin, weak even at the core, where Elite Movers (1,171 reviews) and Dan the Moving Man (878) dominate. The profile pointing at the homepage and the review desert are the binding constraints. No on-page work overcomes a 22-versus-1,171 review gap, so this is foundational.
How
In GBP Manager, change the website link to the Eau Claire page. Eau Claire service and suburb pages are already in production, so point them at the Eau Claire hub and the Chippewa Valley towns to bring it to the depth of the Hudson and Bloomington pages rather than ordering new ones. Reviews are a client lever (outside our standard delivery scope), so flag Eau Claire as the priority for the client to drive.
3

Clean up the links and re-aim the content

aim the effort
Daymakers has more links than any rival but weaker authority, because the profile carries spam from the halted off-page program. Prune the spam, 301-redirect the removed Woodbury page, point internal links at the stronger Roseville long-distance page so the two stop competing, and re-aim the monthly page production from zero-traffic suburb pages to the searches that get traffic.

What
Prune the low-quality and spam links and disavow what is toxic; redirect content effort from the zero-volume suburb pages to the generic and metro terms; 301-redirect the removed Woodbury page into the live Woodbury page; and point internal links at the stronger of the two existing Roseville long-distance pages so they stop competing.
Why
Daymakers has more links than any local competitor but weaker authority, because the profile carries spam from the halted off-page program. The suburb pages draw almost nothing. Woodbury's old URL now 404s, but its intent is still covered by a live page, so a 301 into that page recovers the lost value. Long distance carries the highest-value jobs (a $50 to $63 cost-per-click range), and Roseville's long-distance intent is split across two competing pages, ranking only about #10. The same dilution hits the money terms: 20 or more of the site's own pages surface for each of "moving company", "movers", and "movers near me" in Search Console, which is part of why those high-demand terms draw almost no clicks, so consolidation, not more pages, is the lever.
How
  • Audit the backlink profile, and disavow or clean the spam (their link building is not touched).
  • 301 /woodbury-mn-movers/ to the live /recommended-moving-services-in-woodbury-mn/ page (coordinate with the technical audit already in progress, not a separate task).
  • Make /roseville-mn-movers/roseville-long-distance-movers/ (the stronger by Search Console impressions and query coverage) the primary Roseville long-distance page: point the Roseville profile and internal links at it, and give /long-distance-movers-serving-roseville-mn/ a distinct angle so the two stop competing. Both are already built, so do not redirect either. The wider long-distance set splits across about a dozen pages that compete for the same queries, so concentrate internal links on one primary per intent rather than spreading them.
  • Keep the monthly page production, but point the briefs at the generic and service-area terms from the keyword universe instead of new micro-neighborhood pages.
4

Own the high-value specialty and occasion moves

high value, low volume
Give the high-value specialty and occasion moves their own strong pages (piano and gun-safe are already done well; senior and estate, apartment and high-rise, office and commercial, student moves). Low search volume, high intent, worth it on a few jobs a year each.
Learn moreShow less▾
What
Give the high-value specialty and occasion moves a real page each: piano and gun-safe (already done well), senior and estate moves, apartment and high-rise moves, office and commercial relocations, and student moves around the Twin Cities and Eau Claire campuses. Match each to the right location and GBP category.
Why
These do not show big search volume, but the intent and the job value are high: a specialty or long-distance job is worth far more than a basic local move, so even two to five a year from one page pays for it many times over. They also catch the specific and AI-style searches the generic pages miss. This is the service axis to go with the service-area rings in the first strategy, not more zero-traffic suburb pages.
How
  • Start with the specialty moves Daymakers already performs (piano, gun-safe, senior); make each its own strong page tied to the right location and GBP category.
  • Add occasion pages where demand is real: apartment and high-rise, office and commercial, student moves near the campuses.
  • Write each as a unique page, not a templated near-duplicate; a few strong pages beat dozens of thin ones, which is exactly the suburb-page problem.
  • Check each against what is live and what is scheduled in Asana before writing, so nothing duplicates.
  • Judge these by jobs booked, not by traffic.
A note on framing

This is a strong, winnable account. Daymakers already has the reviews, the ratings, and four dominant locations that most rivals lack. The issue is aim (the service-plus-city terms and the core instead of the generic terms and the wider area) and one weak market (Eau Claire). Re-point the existing effort at the searches that drive calls, fix Eau Claire, and the assets already in place start working harder.
