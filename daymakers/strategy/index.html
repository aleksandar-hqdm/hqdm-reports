Daymakers Moving & Storage

What, why, and how for each strategy · 5 locations, MN & WI
Internal · June 2026

The frame

Daymakers is a strong, established five-pin network with one of the largest review bases in its markets (2,265 reviews at 4.9 to 5.0) and four of five locations leading their local map. But the keyword and map data show a clear inversion. They dominate the "{service} {city}" terms that almost nobody searches (movers roseville, local movers lake elmo: 0 to 390 a month) and the area right next to each office, while they lose the generic "movers near me / moving company / local movers" terms that carry the demand (49,000 to 301,000 a month) across the wider service area. Those generic searches are the Maps-pack queries that drive about 88% of the CallRail leads. So the play is not more zero-volume suburb pages (54 already exist and produce about 7 clicks across 90 days). It is: win the generic-term ring across each pin's service area (the Hudson model, already proven), fix Eau Claire from the ground up (its profile points at the homepage and it has 22 reviews against 400 to 700 elsewhere), clean up a spam-dragged link profile, and re-aim content from suburbs to the terms with real demand.

The competitive picture

The moving field here is local and won mostly on reviews and proximity, not content or links. Daymakers has the best ratings (4.9 to 5.0) and more links than any local rival, so it is not a quality problem. The edge losses split into two different causes that need two different fixes:

  • Prominence (reviews), where the gap is real. Eau Claire: Elite Movers (1,171 reviews) and Dan the Moving Man (878) own the market against Daymakers' 25. Roseville: the metro brands out-review Daymakers' 460, Good Stuff (1,810), You Move Me (1,696), College Hunks (2,202), Two Men and a Truck (906), and win the wider area on prominence over proximity.
  • Proximity, where Daymakers already leads on reviews. Bloomington (731) and Lake Elmo (427) out-review their competitors (Minne Movers 210, WellRive 119, Minute Men 104; Two Men 679, Revival 110), so the edge losses there are to physically closer small movers, not stronger profiles. The fix is service-area signals, not reviews.
  • Hudson is the proof. With 635 reviews against a field whose nearest rival has 74, Hudson leads every search across its whole area. Where Daymakers has the review lead, it wins.

Authority twist: Daymakers has the most backlinks of the local field (1,229) but a weaker domain authority than Elite Movers, Dan, and Good Stuff, who rank stronger with a fraction of the links. The profile is dragged down by spam (a likely residue of the halted off-page program). Quantity without quality. Good Stuff also ranks for more keywords (876 vs 657) with content aimed at real metro terms.

Where Daymakers is strong and weak on the map

  • Strong: the branded "{service} {city}" terms and the immediate area around each office. Four of five pins hold near-total top-3 coverage on their branded terms and the grid core. Hudson leads everything, edge to edge.
  • Weak: the generic "near me" terms at the edges. Roseville "moving company" averages #7.6 (core #3.1, edge #9.7) and "local movers" #8.3; Bloomington "movers near me" decays to #8.7 at the edges; Eau Claire is weak everywhere, including the core.
  • Internal note, keep out of the client report: "local movers near me" (about 49,500 searches) does not rank at all on the Bloomington grid, likely a GBP service or category gap. And the removed Woodbury page (/woodbury-mn-movers/) now returns a 404 after being a top-5 ranker through March; it needs a 301, not a deletion.
  • Not the fix: more suburb pages. The 54 programmatic micro-neighborhood pages target near-zero-volume terms and produced about 7 organic clicks across 90 days combined.

The strategies

1

Win the generic searches across each service-area ring

where the calls are
What

Shift the local effort from the branded "{service} {city}" terms (won, near-zero volume) to the generic terms (movers near me, moving company, local movers) across the wider service area of each pin, not just next to the office.

Why

The generic terms carry the demand (49,000 to 301,000 versus 0 to 390 for the branded ones) and they are the Maps-pack queries that drive about 88% of leads. Daymakers ranks at the top for them in each grid core but slips to #5 to #12 at the edges, where a competitor takes the call. Hudson already wins these edge to edge, so it is proven achievable.

How

Apply the Hudson model to Roseville, Lake Elmo, and Bloomington: a strong review base plus a complete city page plus full GBP service-area signals. Extend each profile's service area outward; for the proximity-driven pins (Bloomington, Lake Elmo) this is the main lever. Track the generic terms across the grid, not just the branded ones.

2

Fix Eau Claire from the ground up

the one weak market
What

Repoint the Eau Claire Google profile from the homepage to /eau-claire-wi-movers/, build that page out with the Chippewa Valley suburbs (Chippewa Falls, Altoona, Menomonie), and grow the review base from 22 toward the level of the other markets.

Why

Eau Claire is the weakest pin, weak even at the core, where Elite Movers (1,171 reviews) and Dan the Moving Man (878) dominate. The profile pointing at the homepage and the review desert are the binding constraints. No on-page work overcomes a 25-versus-1,171 review gap, so this is foundational.

How

In GBP Manager, change the website link to the Eau Claire page. Build the page to match the depth of the Hudson and Bloomington pages. Reviews are a client lever (outside our standard delivery scope), so flag Eau Claire as the priority for the client to drive.

3

Clean up the links and re-aim the content

stop the waste
What

Prune the low-quality and spam links and replace them with a small set of genuine local citations; redirect content effort from the zero-volume suburb pages to the generic and metro terms; 301-redirect the removed Woodbury page; add a long-distance moving page tied to the Roseville profile.

Why

Daymakers has more links than any local competitor but weaker authority, because the profile carries spam from the halted off-page program. The 54 suburb pages draw almost nothing. Woodbury was a top-5 ranker and now 404s, throwing away its value. Long distance carries the highest-value jobs (a $50 to $63 cost-per-click range) and Roseville ranks only about #10.

How
  • Audit the backlink profile, disavow or clean the spam, and build a few quality local listings.
  • 301 /woodbury-mn-movers/ to the Lake Elmo page or the recommended-services page.
  • Build and link a long-distance page to the Roseville profile.
  • Pause new suburb pages; point content at the generic and metro terms from the keyword universe.

Next phase and operational

  • Measurement. Leads come from CallRail (connected June 2026), the system of record for phone and form leads. Data begins April 2026; volume runs about 580 to 790 tracked calls a month, roughly 88% from Google Business and Maps. Each profile uses its own tracking number, so the number on a profile differs from the website number by design, not a NAP error; do not "unify" them.
  • GA4 is a cross-check, not the source. GA4 conversion tracking is unreliable here and its history only starts March 2026, so the lead count comes from CallRail. Mirroring CallRail into GA4 as key events is optional measurement hygiene.
  • Reviews are a client lever. Growing reviews sits outside HQDM's standard delivery scope, so it is framed as a recommendation, with Eau Claire as the clear priority.

What to track

  • The generic "near me" terms across each pin's grid (the lead drivers), not just the branded "{service} {city}" terms the network already owns.
  • Eau Claire's review base and map rank as the profile and page fix settles.
  • CallRail call volume and the Google or Maps share, as the source of truth for leads.
  • The city pages' climb in Google search, the signal that the website recovery is in the money pages and not just the blog.

Minor and optional

  • Suburb pages. An active program ships them. Redirect that effort to strengthening the five pins and the generic-term ring rather than adding more neighborhoods.
  • Off-page. Link quantity already leads local rivals, so the gap is quality, not more links. Cleanup beats acquisition here.
A note on framing

This is a strong, winnable account. Daymakers already has the reviews, the ratings, and four dominant locations that most rivals lack. The issue is aim (the branded terms and the core instead of the generic terms and the wider area) and one weak market (Eau Claire). Re-point the existing effort at the searches that drive calls, fix Eau Claire, and the assets already in place start working harder.
