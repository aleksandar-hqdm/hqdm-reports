The short version

Where this brand stands now, and what to focus on next.
  • Strong business: one of the biggest review counts in their markets (2,265 reviews at 4.9 to 5.0 stars) and 4 of their 5 locations sit at #1 on their local map.
  • Healthy, not a recovery. The location pages already rank well. The problem is clutter: duplicate and thin pages competing with the strong ones and holding them back.

Daymakers is a strong, established five-pin network with one of the largest review bases in its markets (2,265 reviews at 4.9 to 5.0) and four of five locations leading their local map. The location pages rank well in their own right (Hudson #2.4, Lake Elmo #2.1, Bloomington #3.5, Roseville #4.5). The problem is structural: clutter and cannibalization sit on top of that healthy core. Each city carries six or more overlapping pages, and about 189 location pages draw impressions but no clicks (thin doorway pages), splitting the signal and dragging the strong pages down. On top of that, the brand wins the service-plus-city terms almost nobody searches and the area next to each office, but loses the generic "movers near me / moving company / local movers" terms that carry the demand across the wider service area, the Maps-pack queries that drive about 88% of the CallRail leads. So the plan is a clean location-by-service matrix (one strong page per city and service, the duplicates consolidated in and the thin pages folded or cleared), with the authority and links concentrated on the pages that rank, the wider "near me" ring won per pin (the proven Hudson model), Eau Claire fixed from the ground up (it has 22 reviews against roughly 420 to 730 elsewhere, and a thin new page), a live map and reviews on every page. Not more suburb pages.

Search volumes are DataForSEO estimates, not literal counts, and localize to each metro. All GBP work is the five managed pins, re-pointing their service areas, not new locations.

The competitive picture

The field is won on reviews and proximity, not content or links. Daymakers has the best ratings and the most links, so its edge losses split into two causes: a real review gap (Eau Claire, Roseville) and physically closer small movers (Bloomington, Lake Elmo).

The moving field here is local and won mostly on reviews and proximity, not content or links. Daymakers has the best ratings (4.9 to 5.0) and more links than any local rival, so it is not a quality problem. The edge losses split into two different causes that need two different fixes:

  • Prominence (reviews), where the gap is real. Eau Claire: Elite Movers (1,171 reviews) and Dan the Moving Man (878) own the market against Daymakers' 22. Roseville: the metro brands out-review Daymakers' 457, Good Stuff (1,810), You Move Me (1,696), College Hunks (2,202), Two Men and a Truck (906), and win the wider area on prominence over proximity.
  • Proximity, where Daymakers already leads on reviews. Bloomington (729) and Lake Elmo (422) out-review their competitors (Minne Movers 210, WellRive 119, Minute Men 104; Two Men 679, Revival 110), so the edge losses there are to physically closer small movers, not stronger profiles. The fix is service-area signals, not reviews.
  • Hudson is the proof. With 635 reviews against a field whose nearest rival has 74, Hudson leads every search across its whole area. Where Daymakers has the review lead, it wins.

Authority twist: Daymakers has the most backlinks of the local field (1,229) but a weaker domain authority than Elite Movers, Dan, and Good Stuff, who rank stronger with a fraction of the links. The profile is dragged down by spam (a likely residue of the halted off-page program). Quantity without quality. Good Stuff also ranks for more keywords (876 vs 657) with content aimed at real metro terms.

  • Strong: the service-plus-city terms and the immediate area around each office. Four of five pins hold near-total top-3 coverage on those terms and the grid core. Hudson leads everything, edge to edge.
  • Weak: the generic "near me" terms at the edges. Roseville "moving company" averages #7.6 (core #3.1, edge #9.7) and "local movers" #8.3; Bloomington "movers near me" decays to #8.7 at the edges; Eau Claire is weak everywhere, including the core.
  • Internal note, keep out of the client report: "local movers near me" (about 49,500 searches) does not rank at all on the Bloomington grid, likely a GBP service or category gap. And the removed Woodbury page (/woodbury-mn-movers/) now returns a 404 after being a top-5 ranker through March; it needs a 301 into the live Woodbury page, not a deletion.
  • Aim, not volume: the programmatic micro-neighborhood pages target near-zero-volume terms and produced about 7 organic clicks across 90 days combined, so keep the same monthly page production but point it at the generic and service-area terms.

What to track

  • The generic "near me" terms across each pin's grid (the lead drivers), not just the service-plus-city terms the network already owns.
  • Eau Claire's review base and map rank as the page-depth and review work settles.
  • CallRail call volume and the Google or Maps share, as the source of truth for leads.
  • The city pages' climb in Google search, the signal that the website recovery is in the money pages and not just the blog.

The strategies

1

Build a clean location and service matrix

the core fix
One canonical, unique page per city and service, with the duplicate and thin pages consolidated into it. The location pages already rank; the clutter around them is what holds them back.
Learn moreShow less▾
What
Define the grid, each pin city by each core service plus Minneapolis, on one URL pattern, and complete each cell with genuinely unique local content. Run a cannibalization audit to pick the one winning page per city and intent, fold the useful content from the duplicates and suburb pages into it, then 301 or noindex the rest.
Why
The location pages rank strongly (Hudson #2.4, Lake Elmo #2.1, Bloomington #3.5, Roseville #4.5), but each city carries six or more overlapping pages and about 189 location pages pull impressions and no clicks (thin doorway pages). The duplicates split the signal and drag the strong pages. Minneapolis alone has about 35,000 impressions sitting on page three or four.
How
Standardize on the nested /city-mn-movers/city-service-movers/ pattern with unique content per cell; consolidate the duplicates into the cell and 301 them (confirm intent with the client first, since much of this looks like a prior programmatic build); standardize the long-distance-by-city pages onto one pattern; and build the Minneapolis hub and its service children.
2

Concentrate the internal linking

free the trapped authority
Point the links at the pages that rank, link the service pages down to each city, mesh nearby locations together, and fix the anchors that point at fragments instead of pages.
Learn moreShow less▾
What
Route nav, footer, and homepage links to the canonical pin pages; add an "in your area" block on each service page linking to its city versions; add sibling links between nearby city and city/service pages; repoint the "long distance" anchor that points at a /moving-services/ fragment to the dedicated long-distance page; and add breadcrumbs with schema.
Why
The service pages are strong but dead-end at the service level, so that authority is trapped at the top instead of feeding the city pages. Links currently flow down from the hub and stop, and some service anchors send users to a section rather than the page built to rank.
How
Drive the down-links and sibling blocks from a reusable city and service list; fix every service anchor to point at its own page; add breadcrumbs in the page template. After the matrix consolidation, route the priority links to the canonical cells, not across the 100-plus variants.
3

Put a map and reviews on every page

local signal on every page
Add a live map and the reviews to each page, tied to the right Google profile, and check how the reviews element pulls its data.
Learn moreShow less▾
What
A reusable map block, the single pin on a location page and all five on a service page, with matching LocalBusiness schema; surface each location's reviews on its pin page with review schema; and audit whether the reviews element reads live and shows the correct counts.
Why
The pages carry a static map image today, not a live one tied to the profile, so it passes no local signal or profile link. The 2,265-review asset is barely shown on the website, where it would lift both ranking signals and conversion.
How
Build one component in the page template, name and phone identical to each profile; pull live GBP reviews tagged by location; and verify the data source so the counts are accurate.
4

Win the wider service area, location by location

where the calls are
Win the popular "near me" searches further from each office, the way Hudson already does, with the specific fix each pin needs.
Learn moreShow less▾
What
Bloomington: add the missing "Moving and storage service" category and build the wider area. Eau Claire: deepen its page with the Chippewa Valley towns (Chippewa Falls, Altoona, Menomonie) and grow reviews; the profile already points to that page. Roseville: win the generic terms at the service-area edges. Lake Elmo: 301 the dead Woodbury page. Hudson: defend the lead.
Why
Each pin owns the searches next to the office but slips on the generic "near me" terms further out, where a competitor takes the call. Bloomington is even unranked for "local movers near me". Hudson already wins these edge to edge (637 reviews, leads its grid), so it is proven. Eau Claire is the one weak market (22 reviews against Elite Movers' 1,171).
How
Per pin as above, with service-area signals the main lever for the proximity-driven pins (Bloomington, Lake Elmo). Reviews at Eau Claire are a client lever, outside our standard scope, flagged as the priority for the client to drive.
A note on framing

This is a strong, winnable account. Daymakers already has the reviews, the ratings, and four dominant locations that most rivals lack. The issue is aim (the service-plus-city terms and the core instead of the generic terms and the wider area) and one weak market (Eau Claire). Re-point the existing effort at the searches that drive calls, fix Eau Claire, and the assets already in place start working harder.

Subject: Getting found for the moving searches that bring in the calls

Hi [first name],

Quick update on where your locations stand in search, and what we are focused on next.

First, the good news: you have the strongest reviews in your markets (over 2,200 of them at 4.9 to 5.0 stars), and four of your five locations already sit at number one on the local map.

That is a genuinely strong position, and most of your competitors do not have it.

The biggest opportunity is in what you rank for. Right now you win the very specific searches that almost nobody types, and you slip on the popular "movers near me" searches that actually bring in the calls, especially a little further out from each office.

Your Hudson location already wins those popular searches across its whole area, so we know it works. The plan is to repeat that pattern at Roseville, Lake Elmo, and Bloomington so each location gets found across its full service area, not just the streets right next to the office.

Alongside that, we are working on a few more things:

  • giving Eau Claire some attention, since it is the one location lagging the others,
  • pointing your monthly page work at the searches people actually use instead of tiny neighborhood pages that nobody finds,
  • tidying up some lower-quality links that have been holding the website back,
  • building out pages for your higher-value moves (specialty items, long-distance, senior and office moves) where one or two jobs a year more than pays for the work.

You will see all of this tracked in your monthly report.

Happy to walk you through any of it on a quick call.
