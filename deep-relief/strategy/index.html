Deep Relief

Win the radius, not the pin
Internal · June 2026
Start here

The short version

Where this brand stands now, and what to focus on next.
  • At their own location they rank at or near the top for almost every service. That part is already won.
  • The real game is the wider neighborhood. Deep tissue, cupping, and sports already win the inner area, and the popular "near me" searches are one push from winning.
  • Their website holds them back, not their reputation. Google likes them on Maps, but the service pages are thin and barely link to each other, so the homepage shows up instead of the right page.
  • Focus: make the service pages fuller and link them together, build a couple of pages people search for (like couples massage), and rebuild the Oak Hill page that was deleted.
  • Some of their best services are not even being tracked. Start watching cupping and sports massage so wins do not slip by.

The frame

At the storefront Deep Relief ranks #1 to #3 on nearly every service, but that is table stakes. The number that matters is the rank averaged across the whole service-area grid, and that is where the real picture lives. Deep tissue, orthopedic, cupping, and sports already own the inner three miles (grid average under 5). The high-volume "near me" terms sit orange across a wide band (position 4 to 10), one push from green. And cupping and sports are untracked. Traffic held through the restructure (leads are up, organic flat), the live menu is healthy, and reviews lead the market. The focus is radius expansion on the services and directions where Deep Relief can take cells, south and west into the residential service area.

The competitive picture

Rivals are not beating Deep Relief at the pin, on reviews, or on links. They win citywide three ways: deeper content, a niche category, and turning conversions into pages. What each does that Deep Relief does not:

  • Sage Blossom Massage (the threat, about 5x the organic on ~2,000 keywords): a clean, siloed content library under /massage-menu/ and /spa-menu/, a dedicated well-optimized page per service, each pulling 100 to 200 keywords versus Deep Relief's 20 to 30. It includes a couples-massage page (Deep Relief has none), gift-certificate, membership, and specials pages that rank, and a /locations/oak-hill/ page aimed at Deep Relief's own backyard. Plus two GBP listings.
  • Lotus Lymphatic ranks #1 for lymphatic on a category-exact GBP (primary category "Lymph drainage therapist"), not on content or links (about 110 keywords). Same mechanism as cold laser. Concede it.
  • Heeling Hands is smaller but rising on a blog that ranks (its #1 page is a blog post) and on precise GBP categories Deep Relief lacks ("Sports massage therapist," "Lymph drainage therapist").
  • Deep Relief owns the pin, the reviews (454 at 4.9, well ahead of the set), and actually runs more backlinks and citations than any of them, yet organic fell. The exposure is content, structure, and categories, not Maps, reviews, or links.

So the counter is not more reviews and not more links. It is to publish and structure like Sage Blossom, fix the GBP categories, and turn conversions (gift certificates, membership) into indexed pages.

Where to put the off-page effort

Put the link effort behind the buried service pages and into content, and keep real citation NAP consistency for Maps. The numbers behind this: Deep Relief already has more referring domains than the rising rivals (105 versus Heeling's 51 and Lotus's 42), about 195 of 220 backlinks land on the homepage rather than the service pages, and 37% are nofollow with 28% high-spam. The link count is high; the rivals grew on content. So the return is in aiming the link effort at the right pages and shifting the rest into content and on-page.

Maps says #1, organic says page two

For most core services Google already ranks Deep Relief #1 to #3 in Maps at the pin, so the local relevance and prominence are proven. Yet the organic listing for the same service sits on page two or worse. That gap is infrastructure, not authority: the homepage absorbs the ranking (it takes 78% of organic clicks) and the service pages are thin and have almost no internal links pointing at them, so Google falls back to the homepage. These are the highest-confidence organic plays, because we are not earning trust Google withheld, we are unblocking rankings it already wants to give.

The strategies

1

Win the grid radius

the growth lever
What

Two moves on the radius: build the money-cluster pages (the high-volume "near me" terms), and strengthen the handful of existing suburb pages that already sit in winnable (orange) positions. The close-in suburb coverage is saturated, so the gain is in sharpening what can win rather than adding more thin pages.

Why

At the pin Deep Relief is #1 to #3, but the grid average is the real measure. The close-in south and southwest cells are already green, so the pin wins them and they need no page. Of the roughly 22 suburb pages already built, most sit in those green areas or too far out to rank; only five sit in the winnable orange ring (Bannockburn and Woodstone Village at about a mile out, Travis Country and Foothills of Barton Creek to the northeast, Barton Creek West to the north), and those five are thin. There are no genuinely uncovered winnable suburbs left, the empty orange cells reverse-geocode to no-demand micro-subdivisions. So the radius upside is the money-term "near me" intent plus sharpening those five pages.

How

Point the new-page slot at money-cluster pages: a net-new Couples Massage in Austin page (a clear gap, pin already #2 in Maps), a Gift Certificates page, and money-intent posts on the clusters DR owns. For suburbs, strengthen the five thin orange-ring pages: Bannockburn, Woodstone Village, Travis Country, Foothills of Barton Creek, and Barton Creek West. Sunset Valley, Shady Hollow, and Tanglewood Forest are already in production. See the task list below for the exact pages.

2

Unblock the buried service pages

the organic lever
What

Give the service pages the two things they are missing: internal links pointing at them, and depth on the page. Both unblock the same buried pages so the service page, not the homepage, ranks for its term.

Why

Internal links first. On the live site every service page carries about 20 menu and footer links but only zero to three in-content links, and the few that exist are a repeated "cupping and insurance" block, not real context. The services hub links to none of the services, and the money pages (deep tissue, prenatal, sports) get almost no contextual links pointing at them, so Google keeps the homepage as the answer (the homepage takes 78% of organic clicks). Then depth. The pages that already rank are the deep ones (the rivals' winning pages run 1,000-plus words); the buried near-wins sit at the bottom of page one or top of page two because they are thin, not because the titles read badly, and Deep Relief has zero AI Overview presence today. Orthopedic proves both: a real, internally linked, deep page that ranks #1.

How

Add in-content links from the homepage and the hub down to each priority service page using the service term as the anchor; cross-link related services (deep tissue to sports and trigger point, prenatal to swedish); link each existing blog to the service it is about; and replace the boilerplate "cupping and insurance" links with ones that fit the page. Then deepen each near-win page to the depth that already ranks, with a structured FAQ block that AI Overviews can pull from.

3

Formalize the untracked services they own

hidden wins
What

Add cupping therapy and sports massage to tracking and reporting, and give each a light content and GBP pass to defend.

Why

Both already own the three-mile radius (cupping grid average 4.2, sports 4.3, mostly green) but neither is in the Local Dominator tracking, so no one is watching or defending them. They are wins hiding in plain sight, surfaced only by looking beyond the tracked set. Orthopedic (4.6) is the same story and is already the #1 organic page.

How

Add cupping and sports to the tracking grid and confirm orthopedic is on it; make sure each has a strong service page and the matching GBP service entries; defend with the normal monthly cadence.

4

Standardize the service-page structure

consistency · verify live first
What

Put service pages on one template: consistent schema (Service plus FAQ plus BreadcrumbList), a consistent heading outline, and a minimum content depth.

Why

From the archived pages, schema and depth are inconsistent (service schema on some, none on others, no breadcrumbs, depth from 200 to 1,800 words), and the deep pages are the ones that rank. This was read from archived copies because the live site blocks crawlers, so confirm on a couple of live pages before rolling it out.

How

Confirm the current state on two live pages, build one canonical template, roll it across the service pages, fix the pages with no schema, and bring the thin rankers up to the depth that already wins.

5

Wire GA4 lead attribution

measurement
What

Fire the lead events in GA4 (Holbie intake form, Meevo membership and e-gift clicks) so organic-to-lead attribution sits in one place.

Why

Leads are healthy and rising and are already tracked through CallRail, Holbie, and Meevo, so this is not a crisis. But GA4 has zero conversion events, so we cannot see which channel and which pages drive them without manual export. Wiring it makes the radius work measurable.

How

Wire form_submit and the Meevo click events, mark them as Key Events, keep phone and GBP clicks tracked separately, and reconcile to the booking system.

6

Tighten the GBP and the booking loop

the lead engine
What

Fix the booking button, complete and de-duplicate the services list, add the secondary categories they qualify for, and turn gift-voucher redemptions into footfall.

Why

The profile is the lead engine and several things are loose. The "Book online" action is empty (the Meevo link only sits in the contact field). The services list has three duplicate pairs (craniosacral, deep tissue, lymphatic) and is missing services they offer and rank for (thai, trigger point, orthopedic, swedish, cold laser, cryotherapy, couples). They hold only three categories, yet they are #1 in Maps for sports massage with no "Sports massage therapist" category. And they sell Meevo e-gifts, but the person the gift is bought for is never pointed to the location.

How

Set the Book Online button to the Meevo link; remove the duplicate services and add the missing ones; add "Sports massage therapist" as a secondary category and evaluate "Massage spa" for the couples and gifting intent (only categories they genuinely fit, since wrong ones can hurt); and wire the gift-voucher redemption to surface the location, a "get directions" prompt, and a post-visit review ask, so gifts bought for other people become real direction requests and visits.
