The short version

Where this business stands now, and what to focus on next.
  • Their business runs on Google. About 9 in 10 new-customer calls come straight from the Google Business Profile, so the map is the engine.
  • New calls dipped from a strong last fall (about 190 a month) to a steady 50 to 68 a month. Visibility is still good, so this is about strengthening what they have, not starting over.
  • The different phone numbers on Google, the website, and social are the call-tracking system, not a mistake. Every number rings the same main line.
  • The biggest wins are the Google profile (clean the name, reply to reviews, keep them coming) and making the website tap-to-call on every page.
  • Two tracking fixes matter: the website analytics stopped recording when the site was rebuilt in March (the tracking tag did not carry over to the new site; dev has now restored it), and calls are not yet tagged as real jobs versus spam. With the tag back, tagging the calls is what is left to make the report match what the owner hears on the phone.

The frame

Triad runs on its Google Business Profile (about 92% of new calls). Calls dipped from a strong fall, but visibility is still strong, so the plan is to win more of the map, make the website easy to call, point content at local customers, and fix the broken measurement.

The call tracking is clear: about 92% of first-time calls come straight through the Google Business Profile, with most of the rest from Google search. The website form is a small add-on. New-customer calls peaked last fall (about 190 a month in October and November), then settled to a steady 50 to 68 a month through 2026. That is a real dip, but it is a dip in map-driven calls, not a website collapse: the site's search visibility held over the same window. Triad still ranks top 3 for the core "electrician in Greensboro" searches in the north and central parts of town, with a strong 4.8 rating on 128 reviews. So the play is not to start over. It is to win more of the map (the profile and reviews), make the website easy to call and tuned for local searches, point new content at Greensboro customers instead of national DIY readers, and fix the two measurement gaps so the numbers can be trusted.

The competitive picture

No competitor owns the whole map; different shops lead in different parts of town. The clearest gap versus the busiest rival is review count and profile activity, not the service or the site.

The Greensboro electrician map is fragmented. Across the local grid, different profiles take the #1 spot in different areas (Mr. Electric, Meyler, Jerry's, and Triad each lead somewhere), so no one runs away with it and the rest of the map is winnable. Where competitors are ahead, it is on profile prominence, mainly reviews:

  • Mr. Electric of Greensboro (393 reviews, 4.9): the most reviews in town and a leader across much of the map; the profile to defend against.
  • Balex Electrical (165, 4.8) and Dr. Sparky (157, 5.0): strong review bases that help them hold map spots.
  • Welborn Electric (89, 4.6), Precision Electric (54, 5.0), Meyler Electric (30, 4.9): smaller profiles that still take cells near their own locations.
  • Triad (128 reviews, 4.8): a strong rating and a real review base, top 3 in the north and central grid. The gap to the leader is review volume and profile activity, not service quality or the website.

Pattern: locally, the lead lever is profile prominence (reviews and activity), not content or links. Triad is competitive and well-rated; closing the review gap and keeping the profile active is what widens its map coverage.

Measurement and the phone numbers

The different phone numbers are call tracking working as designed. The two real gaps are the website analytics that broke in the rebuild and calls that are not yet tagged as real jobs.
Learn moreShow less▾

The phone numbers the client flagged are the call-tracking setup, not an error. The Google profile shows a tracking number (336-962-7549) that forwards to the real line (336-499-2015), and the website uses a small pool of rotating numbers that all forward to the same line. Every number a customer can dial rings the main business line, and the tracking is how we prove which calls Google and the website send. The cleanup is cosmetic: show the real number consistently on the website and social, and list it as a second number on the profile.

Two real gaps to close

  • The website's analytics recorded almost nothing from the March rebuild onward (0 sessions in the 30 days before the fix, against about 600 the month before) because the tracking tag did not carry over to the rebuilt site. Dev has now re-added the tag, so visit reporting resumes from here; the rebuild-to-now gap stays empty, but no leads were lost. The earlier data is also inflated by overseas bot traffic, so filter that out now that data is flowing again. Leads never stopped; the call tracking ran the whole time.
  • Calls are not tagged as real jobs versus sales calls, wrong numbers, and repeat callers (only a handful have ever been marked). Turning on tagging lets the monthly report show genuine new leads, which is what the owner counts when they say "only one was a new lead."

The strategies

1

Win back Google Maps calls

the lead engine
Strengthen the profile that drives about 92% of calls: clean the name, keep reviews flowing and reply to them, complete the services list, and list the real number as a second line.
Learn moreShow less▾
What
Clean the Google Business Profile name to "Triad Electric Solutions"; keep a steady review flow and reply to every review (the client's team); complete the services list; add the real line as a second number.
Why
About 92% of new calls come from the profile, so this is the single highest-value area. The live Google name carries a keyword tail ("- Electrician Greensboro NC") that breaks Google's naming rules and a competitor could report it. Reviews and profile activity are the prominence signals that widen map coverage: Triad sits at 128 reviews versus the busiest rival's 393, and none of its reviews have a reply. These moves are cheap, high-return, and never touch the website.
How
In the profile manager: rename to the clean business name; have the team ask every happy customer for a review and reply to each one; complete the services list with short descriptions; add +1-336-499-2015 as an additional number while keeping the tracking number primary so calls stay measured.
2

Make the website easy to call and easy to find

conversion + on-page basics
Add tap-to-call on every page and put the business details on the homepage so Google reads them.
Learn moreShow less▾
What
Add tap-to-call to the homepage and every service page; add the business details (LocalBusiness) to the homepage; add the missing headings.
Why
Most visitors are on a phone, ready to call, but the displayed number is not a tap-to-call link in the page itself on the homepage or the service pages (only the contact page has it), which loses mobile callers. The homepage carries only basic markup, so Google cannot cleanly read the business name, address, phone, and hours. Several service and area pages are missing their main heading. These are quick fixes to pages that already exist.
How
  • Wrap the displayed phone number in a tap-to-call link across the whole site, and keep a clear "Call now" button near the top of every page.
  • Add LocalBusiness details to the homepage (name, address, phone, hours, location), matching the markup the service pages already have.
  • Add the missing H1 headings to the service and area pages that lack one.
3

Point content at Greensboro customers, not DIY readers

the content axis
The blog's older posts mostly rank for national how-to questions. The content already in production has shifted to local Greensboro topics, which is the right move, so keep it going, make each piece unique, and refresh the old posts toward local intent.
Learn moreShow less▾
What
Keep the local Greensboro content already in production, give each neighborhood page genuine local depth (they are unique today but run thin, around 500 words), and refresh the older national how-to posts toward local intent.
Why
The site's strongest-ranking pages are older national DIY how-tos ("how to splice wires", "how a generator makes electricity"), and in those rankings the site competes with Home Depot and DIY publishers, not local electricians. That traffic reads and leaves; it does not call. The good news is the current content pipeline has already turned local (new Greensboro neighborhood pages and topics like lighting and outdoor wiring), which is the right direction. The job now is to keep each piece unique and locally specific, and to refresh the legacy how-to posts so they earn local searches too.
How
Keep new posts on Greensboro service and neighborhood intent (for example "panel upgrade cost in Greensboro", "EV charger installer in [neighborhood]"), and refresh the older how-to posts the same way; link each piece to the service pages, keep one clear call-to-action per page, and judge each by calls booked, not page views.
4

Fix the measurement so we can prove it

measurement
Website reporting is restored (dev re-added the tag that dropped in the March rebuild); the rest of the measurement work is to filter bot traffic and tag real job leads so the report matches the phone.
Learn moreShow less▾
What
The Google Analytics tag has been re-added to the rebuilt site, so visits record again (it had stopped firing in March); next, filter the bot and spam traffic, and turn on lead tagging in the call tracking.
Why
The website's analytics property recorded almost nothing from the March rebuild until dev re-added the tag this month (0 sessions in the 30 days before the fix, against about 600 the month before the rebuild), because the tracking tag did not carry over to the new site, and the earlier data is inflated by overseas bot traffic, so it is only now becoming a reliable read again. Calls are not tagged, so we cannot separate real jobs from sales calls and wrong numbers, which is exactly why a slow day can look like "only one real lead". Fixing both makes the numbers trustworthy and matches what the owner sees on the phone.
How
Dev has re-added the Google Analytics tag to the rebuilt site; confirm it is firing on every page and that visits are recording again, then add bot filtering so overseas spam is excluded; in the call tracking, tag first-time calls as job or not-a-job so the monthly report shows qualified new leads, not raw call counts.
