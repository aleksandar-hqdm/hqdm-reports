Where to send each service cluster

The same booths from the menu, pointed at the Miami areas where that kind of event is busiest. The chip shows how visible MIA is there today, so the gaps are the priorities.

Where to point the effort

The high-potential coast (Miami Beach, Bal Harbour, Aventura, Key Biscayne) and center (Wynwood, Brickell, Downtown) are not yet won, and several are not even in the tracking grid. Coral Gables is the closest high-value area to a win. These are the areas the strategy moves the map tracking and content toward.

The cheapest first move

The Google profile is still set up as a photography studio rather than a photo booth company. Fixing the category is what makes MIA eligible to rank in all of these areas in the first place. It is the first item in the work plan on the performance report.

The map shades each part of Miami by event demand, a read of where the wedding, corporate and party venues cluster (hotels, ballrooms, waterfront and rooftop venues, banquet halls), since local search counts are too small to rank on. The toggle flips the same map to MIA's live Google Maps position for any search. The service-cluster targets below use the same demand read. Both point the same way: the events sit on the coast and center, where MIA is least visible.
