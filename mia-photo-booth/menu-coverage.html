Why this helps bookings

Most visitors land on a guide article, not a booking page. An occasion-first menu gives a wedding or corporate visitor a clear next step in one click, which is exactly the gap the performance report flags. The booth names stay, they just sit under the event the customer came for.

How to read this map

Color shows where each kind of event is busiest across Miami (darker means more potential). The dot on each area shows how visible MIA already is on Google Maps there. Switch the lens to see weddings, corporate or parties on their own. The pattern is the point: the busiest event areas are the coast and center, and that is exactly where MIA is least visible today.

Where to send each service cluster

The same booths from the menu, pointed at the Miami areas where that kind of event is busiest. The chip shows how visible MIA is there today, so the gaps are the priorities.

Where to point the effort

The high-potential coast (Miami Beach, Bal Harbour, Aventura, Key Biscayne) and center (Wynwood, Brickell, Downtown) are not yet won, and several are not even in the tracking grid. Coral Gables is the closest high-value area to a win. These are the areas the strategy moves the map tracking and content toward.

The cheapest first move

The Google profile is still set up as a photography studio rather than a photo booth company. Fixing the category is what makes MIA eligible to rank in all of these areas in the first place. It is the first item in the work plan on the performance report.

Potential is a planning estimate based on where Miami's wedding, corporate and party venues actually cluster (hotels, ballrooms, waterfront and rooftop venues, banquet halls), not a count of searches. Local search volumes for these areas are too small to rank on reliably. Visibility comes from the Local Dominator Google Maps scans, latest reading, best position across the main commercial searches. Areas marked "not yet tracked" sit outside the current scan grid, which currently leans inland and west.
